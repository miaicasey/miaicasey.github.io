Custom Headers, Footers and Signatures

Every document Heidi exports carries the clinic's own letterhead and signature.

Context

Clinical documents leave Heidi: printed, faxed, emailed to GPs, handed to patients. The moment they left they looked like they came from nowhere. Twelve enterprise accounts covering 800 or more users had asked for letterheads, a 250-seat enterprise wanted footer banners, a hospital network asked for template-level letterheads, and sales described the gap as a churn risk.

Problem

A V1 editor sat in review with a structural problem: single-pass PDF export could not place footers on multi-page documents. Ship a letterhead and signature system that works across every export path, for solo clinicians and org admins.

Goals and measures

Export parity across PDF, Word, Print and Email; retention and export conversion of clinicians on the new editor against control; the enterprise asks closed.

Discovery

I took the project over mid-year, pulled the demand evidence together (Canny requests, sales notes, the hospital network’s ask) and structured the work into three milestones: editor, signatures and PDF export; template-level application, export preview, org controls and export parity; ad hoc letterhead on the Scribe canvas.

Explorations and wireframes

I wireframed the entry point as a prompt to upload an example letter so Heidi extracts the practice details, the editor with header, footer and signature blocks, the library, and the template-level application that shows the letterhead on the canvas during editing. The Product Designer took those to final.

Design collaboration and QA

I ran design QA against the build, wrote the ticket set with the two engineers, and owned the release-blocking analytics events to closure. The image bank flow for header uploads and the sidebar transparency during lookup came out of that QA round.

Build and rollout

V2 went to 50%, then 75, then 100% with user comms, holding back UK, EU and the enterprise accounts CS flagged. Export parity, the settings shortcut and a “Recommended” tag on PDF shipped with it.

Impact

  • Exporting clinicians on the new editor retained at 85% in week one against 79% for control, and the gap held at 6 to 9 points through week four.
  • Export conversion ran about 2% higher over the same window.
  • Both reads came from the staged rollout window. Once the flag hit 100% the remaining control group was UK, EU and managed users, so the clean comparison closed there. The demand evidence stands on its own.

Reflection

The feature with the most enterprise demand on the board had a V1 stuck in review on a PDF rendering problem. Structuring it into three milestones and making the analytics events a release blocker turned it into something I could ship in stages and read.

I would change the control design. Once the flag hit 100% the comparison closed, and the clean read lives in a three-week window. A holdout cohort inside the launch markets would have given a durable number instead of a directional one. I would also bring the enterprise accounts customer success flagged into the rollout sooner, under a template-level inheritance they controlled, rather than hold them back.

I carry forward the habit of writing the ticket set with the engineers rather than for them. The image bank flow and the sidebar transparency both came out of QA conversations, and neither would have appeared in a spec I wrote alone.
