My contribution

I scoped the new-user journey around reaching a first consult and a first note. I explored parts of the flow in Figma and through coded prototypes, translated the decisions into requirements for design and engineering, and reviewed the resulting experience from a UX perspective. I also helped coordinate stakeholder input, QA and measurement so the team could assess the journey after launch.

Context and opportunity

New signups completed a metadata step (country, role, specialty) and dropped into the product. The one demo was passive: users watched a sample transcript play rather than trying the workflow. The metric that mattered was the share of signups creating a first note.

The problem to solve

Get a new clinician to record a first consult and read a first note before they leave onboarding.

Success criteria

Transcription start, first note and activation within 14 days against a matched control, with events on every step.

Discovery and prioritisation

I took the handover, audited the old flow’s drop-off by step, and set the hypothesis: a guided first session that does the workflow rather than describing it.

Scope and requirements

I wireframed the new sequence (Scribe integrated into note creation, demo transcript and note personalised by specialty, book-a-demo step, regional compliance step, inline note editing, referral letter link) and reviewed the PromptTeams specialty templates myself before they went in.

Cross-functional delivery and quality

The contract designer and the Product Designer designed the steps from the wireframes; I ran QA on each build and on a Senior Product Designer’s three-screen variant that followed, and caught the SSO redirection creating duplicate accounts, which led to a process rule that identity and growth get looped in before anyone changes mandatory signup fields.

Launch and rollout

Shipped to English markets first and to Spain, France and DACH after localisation. The first read I posted (44 against 37% to first note) did not survive scrutiny: the control completion event was missing on about 37,000 completions and the cohorts were 3,200 against 99,000. I found that, wrote the fix tickets, and reported the standalone numbers instead. The dashboard family I built (v3, v3.2 per country, v4 per country) became the onboarding source of truth.

Results and evidence

Across about 59,000 control signups and 33,000 V3 signups in English-speaking markets, excluding Heidi staff:

  • 52% of V3 signups start a transcription in their first two weeks, against 48% on the old flow. At the V3 cohort size that is about 1,250 more clinicians recording a first consult.
  • 58% generate a first note in two weeks, against 57%, about 560 more first notes.
  • Onboarding completion within a day edged up from 98.7 to 99.2%.
  • First-week activation moved from 58 to 59% across everyone who started.
  • Clinicians who finish the guided flow retain 3 to 4 points above those who finished the old flow, every week through week four. The two completion events differ, so I read that as supporting evidence rather than proof.
  • Template creation and demo booking read lower on V3, which follows from moving both steps out of the flow, and upgrades read lower on a cohort that has had less time to convert. I leave those out of the case on purpose.

Reflection

The first number I posted was wrong. The control arm had no matching events, the cohorts were an order of magnitude apart, and I had already shared the lift in the pod channel. Finding that myself, writing the fix tickets and reporting the smaller, defensible number is the part of this project I would show someone who asked how I work.

I would ship matched control events with the feature rather than after it, and I would treat the SSO duplicate-account bug as a prerequisite rather than a follow-on, because it skewed the first cohort. I would also test the demo step on its own before bundling it into the flow. The first-hour retention lift was the clearest signal and deserved a cleaner read.

The dashboard family became the pod’s source of truth for onboarding, and that is the outcome I value most: the team reads from the same numbers I do.

Next steps

The next step is to use the first-consult-to-first-note funnel and cohort evidence to identify the highest-friction step. Reliable instrumentation and qualitative feedback should guide the next onboarding iteration.
