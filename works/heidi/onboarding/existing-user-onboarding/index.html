Existing user onboarding (Heidi 2)

Landing 60,000 existing clinicians in a new product with a demo built on their own specialty.

Context

Heidi 2 replaced the home surface every existing clinician knew. Two weeks before launch the onboarding for those users sat with the Agents team with little progress, no dependencies sourced, and no shared view of what a returning clinician should see first.

Problem

Land tens of thousands of existing clinicians in a new product, in their own clinical language, and get them to a first message in one sitting.

Goals and measures

Share of intro viewers who send a Heidi 2 message within a day; demo-to-message conversion; demo requests routed to sales.

Discovery

I pulled dashboards, guidance and feature requests from across the business, sourced the dependencies from legal, localisation and sales, and aligned the Agents PM and the Head of Agent Engineering on scope in Linear. In check-ins with the Engineering Lead, I worked through engineering resourcing and helped the team reprioritise around the launch. I raised the last-minute call to launch in French and briefed design and engineering on localisation practice before the build landed.

Explorations and wireframes

I wireframed the four-step sequence (welcome, specialty-personalised hero demo, animated carousel of the apps, spotlight tour) and the “Get started” checklist, and wrote the specialty framework for the demo content so a GP and a psychologist each saw their own task. The framework could adapt to another specialty or language without redesigning the flow, allowing the French rollout to inherit the same structure.

Design collaboration and QA

Design iterated the frames from the wireframes; I reviewed each round for the copy by specialty, the medical device onboarding copy requirements and the localisation gaps, and QA’d the working build before sign-off.

Build and rollout

The working build landed a week after sign-off and shipped with the first Heidi 2 rollout step. The checklist routes real book-a-demo requests to sales through the direct booking flow I built in Q2, which saves a qualifying call per lead.

Impact

  • Around 61,000 existing clinicians saw the Heidi 2 intro in the first six weeks, and one in four sent a Heidi 2 message within a day. The first fortnight read was 21% on about 20,000 users, so the intro kept converting as it reached the long tail.
  • Clinicians who clicked “See how it works” converted to a first message at 40%, double the rate of those who skipped the demo.
  • About 1,400 existing clinicians a month click “Book a demo” from the intro, and each one reaches sales without a qualifying call.
  • Heidi 2 active users per week grew from under 1,000 the week before launch to over 20,000 four weeks after. The intro is one of several entry points, so that number belongs to the launch rather than to me.

Reflection

Two weeks from launch this sat with another team and had not moved. The lesson I took is that alignment is product work: pulling dashboards, legal dependencies and sales asks into one scope did more for the launch than any single screen.

If I did it again I would build the specialty copy framework before the generic version rather than after. The demo built on the clinician’s own specialty is what doubled first-message conversion, and the generic pass cost a week we could have spent there. I would also instrument the demo click sooner. The fall in demo clicks as the rollout reached returning users was obvious in hindsight and would have been useful live.

The shape of the number stays with me: one in four existing clinicians sent a message within a day of seeing the intro, on a product that replaced the home screen they knew.
