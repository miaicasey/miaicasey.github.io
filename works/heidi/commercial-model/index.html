Product Manager / Pricing and monetisation

Heidi 2 commercial model

Moving Heidi from flat plans to usage-based seats, with the first live price test on real clinicians.

Context

Heidi 2 moved the product to usage-based seats: every seat carries an allowance each month and a Max tier gives you more of it. Five weeks out nobody knew what a heavy clinician would pay for five times the usage or how limits should surface without feeling like a wall. The Growth PM and Principal Product Designer were on leave and the commercial model itself was still moving.

Problem

Get a live price signal from real clinicians before launch, and design usage visibility and upgrade surfaces that fit the Heidi 2 launch window.

Goals and measures

Three price arms live to the beta cohort with real charges; an upgrade modal conversion rate; a read on how buyers behave relative to their usage; and components ready for the launch design system.

Discovery

I audited frontier lab upgrade patterns in FigJam as prior art, pulled scattered Slack context into Linear projects and an initiative, and wrote five milestone gates with go and no-go criteria: designs and copy locked, smoke test live, price decision from the read, launch with usage visible, enforcement ready.

Explorations and wireframes

I wireframed the usage progress bar, the nudge banners at 50, 75, 90 and 100%, and the upgrade modal for every tier, then handed them to the Product Designer as the brief. The copy decisions sat with me and commerce: Clinician Standard and Max, “usage” over “credits”, “5x more usage”, no “agent” in copy.

Design collaboration and QA

The Product Designer built the components from the wireframes and we iterated on the modal’s two-panel layout until the trade read in one line. I used the launch to retire the off-brand purple upgrade colour; we landed on a single blue and the design systems designer shipped it through the design system a week before launch. I QA’d the production build with two engineers across Scribe Plus, Team users and non-default currencies.

Build and rollout

Clinician Max beta went live to production with three price arms (269, 349 and 429 dollars) to about 1,000 agent beta users and a Datadog dashboard watching it. I ran the analysis that fed the price decision, then co-owned the opt-in release with the Engineering Lead: consent modal with cohort variants, awareness banner, complimentary usage surfaces, and the staged rollout from 25% of new free signups to 100%.

Impact

  • Explicit upgrade modals convert at 12 to 15% of the clinicians who see them.
  • 43% of Clinician buyers used 10 or fewer credits in the month before paying, so buyers convert on trial expiry and features rather than on hitting a wall.
  • A 150-credit tier covers 80% of Clinician user-months; about 13,500 free users a month sit above 30 credits.
  • These figures come from the smoke test analysis and the billing dashboard rather than a live warehouse pull.

Reflection

I picked this up with no designs and no context, five weeks from launch, and the thing that made it work was drawing the whole surface before briefing anyone. The designer had a sketch of the usage bar, the nudges and the modal on day one, and we locked the components in a week.

I would change two things. I would have argued for the price arms to run longer before the decision, because a week of smoke test on a thousand beta users is thin ground for a pricing call. And I would have had the warehouse tag the Max seat from the first charge, so anyone could pull the live numbers later without a finance query.

The habit I keep is treating a pricing question as a measurement problem first. The useful output was knowing that buyers convert on trial expiry and features, and that a 150-credit tier covers most of them. The modal was the vehicle for finding that out.
