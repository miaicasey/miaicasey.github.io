Community Routines

Routines with a clinician's name on them, so the next clinician trusts the supply.

Context

Heidi’s template community was a library in the strict sense: unattributed artefacts, no creator identity, no signal of who used what. Heidi 2 launched routines (tasks Heidi runs for you on a schedule), and an empty or anonymous routine library on launch day would have told clinicians nobody they trusted was using them.

Problem

Put clinician-attributed routines on the shelf for launch, with a trust signal on every card, without building committee infrastructure first.

Goals and measures

About 30 clinician-branded routines live at launch, reviewed by a clinician, with a seeding funnel and a review pipeline that survive past launch day.

Discovery

The inherited plan had four phases (committee, waitlist, launch event, LinkedIn seeding). I cut it to the supply problem and sourced routines myself by reaching out to beta users and internal clinicians, then stood up the submissions database in Notion and the review pipeline with the clinical knowledge team.

Explorations and wireframes

I wireframed the routine card with name, specialty and usage count, the chat-first routine page, and the Discover categories led by general clinician routines, and wrote the attribution model (fork-on-edit keeps the original author visible).

Design collaboration and QA

The Product Designer took the card and page designs to final and shipped PR 24151 rendering approved routines for seven specialties. I vibe-coded the Discover category logic (PR 24211) and QA’d the attribution and specialty filters against the review database.

Sharing mechanism

A useful routine should be easy to pass to a colleague. I scoped the sharing mechanism around that moment: share from the product in seconds, then give the recipient enough context to understand and use the routine. The first milestone was narrowed to clinician-to-clinician link sharing; the broader social and team-sharing designs remain later work.

The proposed flow defaults external sharing of a clinician’s own routine to “Share with community”. Leaving it on sends the routine into the submission and moderation flow; it does not publish it automatically. Turning it off keeps the routine accessible by link without putting it in community discovery or search.

A signed-in recipient lands on the specific routine with its detail drawer open beside the library. A recipient without an account goes through login or signup, then returns to that same routine. Adding it creates an editable copy in their own collection, so changes do not alter the community version.

The designs below explore the share preview, editable caption, access controls and copy-link action across four visual treatments. Channel-specific social cards, image downloads, QR entry and team administration are follow-on concepts, rather than claims about the first release. Sharing was scoped and queued behind the initial routines work at this stage.

Build and rollout

Growth handed the build to the Agents team after launch. I scoped sharing, public submission and an automated approval flow (safety evals auto-publish Safe, escalate Unsafe to a clinician) across nine tickets and handed those to the Agents PM and the Engineering Lead.

Impact

  • 34 routines in the submissions database at launch, 22 approved, 18 from the Heidi team and 4 from external clinicians.
  • Launched a week after Heidi 2, which I own: I moved on it too late once the commercial model and onboarding were under control.

Reflection

Cutting a four-phase committee plan down to thirty named routines and sourcing them myself was the right call. Supply on launch day did more than infrastructure would have.

I own the week it slipped. I moved on it after the commercial model and onboarding were under control, and in hindsight the three could have run in parallel with an earlier hand-off to the Agents team. I would also set up the review pipeline with the clinical team before sourcing routines rather than alongside it, because approvals became the bottleneck once submissions arrived.

The thing I keep is the attribution model. Fork-on-edit with the original author visible is a small decision that makes a community library trustworthy, and I would make it on day one of anything similar.
