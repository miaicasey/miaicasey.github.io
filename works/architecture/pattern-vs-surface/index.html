ARCHITECTURE / MODULE 02

Translating the flow of terrain and draped fabric into grids, panels and a hybrid surface.

Architecture & spatial design

TOOLS & METHODS

  • Rhino
  • Terrain analysis
  • Grid studies

Original study

Context

In my analysis, I wanted to capture the major lines of flow shared between the sample and terrain analysis. Through my experimentations within my grid studies I was able to manipulate the same flow using curve attractors and by manipulating the magnitude. This was largely achieved within my grid study.

Terrain analysis

Analysis of a section of the Tasmanian terrain, annotated with different colors to draw parallels to the fabric analysis

Fabric analysis

Analysis of a section of Raphael’s ‘The Alba Madonna’, annotated with different colors to draw parallels to the terrain analysis

Design process

Continuing I worked on experimenting with different heights and angles as well as multiple try angles. In my panel study, I was able to achieve this quite well. Landing on panel 7 where I used a combination of 5 different triangles to better encapsulate the movement in my fabric and the height in my terrain.

The hybrid then spoke to both the grid and the panel. Although it was difficult to achieve a fine balance between both studies. Through trial and error, I was able to create a combination that was not only developable but achieved the intensity I desired.

Moving on with the artifact and composite. I manipulated some of the blocks by lengthening and changing the direction they were pointing towards to speak better with the flow of the fabric as seen. Focusing especially on the bottom right where I suggest a continuation.

Here you can see the changes in design through the development stages. Note how the combination of the grid and panel study enables the hybrid to mimic the flow and folds of the fabric and terrain samples

Iteration

Here you can see the different iterations of my grid and panel studies where I experimented with a range of tools such as curve attractors, point attractors, and magnitudes…

This process of trial and error enabled me to be well acquainted with Rhino and its tools as well as learn about not only the conventional way to use them but also unconventional ways which enable me to achieve a desired result more efficiently and effectively.

Reflection

Overall this was a really hard task for me as I’ve never used Rhino before but I enjoyed it a lot especially creating the composite. Learning how these processes create the sum of the final product really enhanced the learning experience.

I’ve also found it really interesting how the design process is consistently evolving. I found myself oscillating between focusing on not only the flow but the lighting and the interaction between elements going to show how important consideration of different elements is in the final product. I found that distance directly impacts depth and so do shadows when they work together with lines.

I like the bottom right area the most as It allows the viewer to continue to imagine the 3-dimensional space beyond the drape and artifact. Into a realm that is seemingly out of bounds.
