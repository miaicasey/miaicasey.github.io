Frame vs Field

ARCHITECTURE / MODULE 03

A spatial interpretation of Zirma from Italo Calvino’s Invisible Cities, using Rhino and Photoshop.

DISCIPLINE

Architecture & spatial design

TOOLS & METHODS

  • Rhino
  • Adobe Photoshop
  • Literary analysis
  • Perspective collage
Original study

Context: frame and field

Reflecting on this task, Frame has a larger focus on utilitarianism providing a blueprint or foundation upon which the field can be built upon and explored. It offers a structural framework that supports and defines the boundaries of a space. And provides, arguably, a more concrete objective background to the more subjective experience of the field. Field on the other hand is more flexible and broad, it’s created through interaction with the frame and relates more to the human experience, and provides context for the Frame.

Text analysis

In reading later chapters it can be noted that all the cities although seemingly different are all depictions of Venice. Thus, Zirma seems to suggest through its nuance and palimpsestic repetition that it is ‘memory’ and mind that form cities. Furthermore suggesting that it is our perceptions based off our subjective experiences that shape the world we experience and mold our personal experiences into a lens as to perceive our cities and moreover, our world. Begging the question of whether we can truly experience our reality for what it is and if we shape our environment or our environment shapes us. My project thus takes this idea and tries to mediate between these paradoxical viewpoints and inhabits a hetero-topic liminal space in which both perspectives can coexist.

Perspective 1: Sailors

To pay homage to the book and its references to Venice as well as the chapter which depicts ideas of a heterotopia. I decided to select the characters within my scene from artistic depictions of the Venice carnival. From pieces such as ‘a portrait of Venice unmasked‘ by Guardi.  Additionally, with the mention of sailors and their symbolic intertwine with travelers I decided to set the scene in a sea storm. Incorporating vessels to represent the dirigibles mentioned in the text. I also wanted to make references to iconic symbols such as yin and yang as a representation of mediation and balance as well as reemphasize the theme of perception by experimenting with light.

Furthermore I added textures and mist to create an aura of mystery. If you look closely at the waves I used a white sea mist texture to heighten the sense of intensity within the composition as well as guide the viewers eye to the moon which is secluded by the structure. Additionally I incorporated a stream of bats which symbolize rebirth and luck, flying towards the feminine moon. It can be noted that they are also glanced at by the subject on the left. Although subtle I also added cracking textures around some of the marble stones to hint at the transience of perception and memory.

Perspective 2: Ozymandias

Inspired by Percy Shelley’s Ozymandias and his wife’s more notable book Frankenstein (Mary Shelley) which is also known as the modern Prometheus. I drew inspiration from both particularly their warnings in regard to the pursuit of knowledge. Frankenstein is a more shocking horror novel that demonstrates the dangers of perceiving the world as one where progression, honor, and glory are the highest forms of success. Whilst Ozymandias challenges the idea as an anecdote where all of these credits fall into decay and ruin as the passing of time erodes and lays fine dust over our histories.

I find that it is quite relevant to Italo Calvino’s Invisible Cities as they both challenge perception and values. They explore the concept of paradoxes and the cyclical nature of life. Thus I created this perspective as a contradiction to my first as well as an anecdote. Tinting it with yellow symbolizes tranquility. However, juxtaposing this tranquility with destruction and decay. Perhaps suggesting that to be at peace in life is to embrace nature death and destruction as one cannot co-exist without the other.

Isometric notation: first iteration

This was my first iteration of which I attempted to use bold lines and coloring to emphasize the flow of the piece. Additionally how direction and time complement each other even when positioned as oppositional forces. I wanted to emphasize the abstract subjectivity in which time is experienced as seen in the text. For example: think of your happiest memory, then reflect on how the passing of time felt; then think of the most boring moment in your life and then reflect and compare differences in which you experience the passing of time. I wanted to capture this undulating nature within my notations. However this is come at a cost of the aesthetic quality of the piece and the balance of the overall composition which I worked on later

Development and reflection

In my second iteration, I attempted to manipulate the elements to create harmony in a way that it would seem as if the elements are speaking to each other. This is predominantly seen in the waves of which time is disrupted within the areas of a heavy atmosphere emphasizing its fluid nature. However, it made the piece appear quite chaotic and the viewer would struggle to know where to look. Thus I added the sectional cut to emphasize the important areas and to also section off the three areas as I outlined in my annotations that represent the different modes of perception. Additionally, I added arrows to question the circularity of repetition as seen in the text and the heterotopia in which Zirma inhabits.

If you continue to flip through you may notice how the time-line disrupts the composition of the piece I decided to review and redesign my annotations to focus on the main idea which is the disruption of time by atmosphere and energy. Additionally, composing the annotations so that it compliments the direction lines. I also wanted to highlight the paradoxical nature of the text through the opposing direction lines which suggests a mediation of opposing forces with time.
